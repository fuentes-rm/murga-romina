## Abstract

The selective separation of molybdenite from copper sulfide concentrate in flotation processes is commonly carried out using sodium hydrosulfide (NaHS) to depress chalcopyrite and permit only the flotation of molybdenite. However, this reagent generates toxic and flammable gas. The objective of this research was to study the feasible application of commercial lignosulfonates (LSs) in the separation by froth flotation of molybdenite and chalcopyrite in seawater as an environmentally friendlier alternative reagent to NaHS. Microflotation, absorbance tests, and zeta potential measurements were performed at pH 8 in seawater and 0.01 M NaCl. The results showed that selective separation of copper and molybdenum is achieved in both aqueous media due to the strong depressant effect on molybdenite and low depression of chalcopyrite at 10 ppm of LSs when the collector (PAX) is added prior to LSs. Absorbance and zeta potential measurements confirmed greater LS adsorption onto molybdenite in seawater due to the high ionic strength of the medium.
