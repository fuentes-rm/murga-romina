## Abstract

Guar gum (GG) was evaluated as a depressant of pyrite and chalcopyrite in the presence of potassium amyl xanthate (PAX) as collector and methyl isobutyl carbinol (MIBC) as a frothing agent. The interaction of this biopolymer with these minerals was monitored in two aqueous systems, seawater (SW) and continental water (CW), through contact angle, zeta potential, ATR-FTIR spectroscopy, and UV-Visible spectroscopy. The ATR-FTIR spectra revealed a characteristic O-H stretching vibration band at around 3500-3000 cm-1, indicating an interaction between the GG and the minerals. Zeta potential measurements confirmed that GG possesses an anionic nature (-4.71 ± 1.24 mV), which enables electrostatic interaction with the mineral surface and the change in the potential of this surface toward more positive values. Contact angle results showed that the addition of GG exacerbates pyrite's hydrophilic character if it is added after the addition of PAX, whereas this is not the case with chalcopyrite, allowing for its flotation. UV-Visible spectra demonstrated that GG reduced PAX adsorption onto pyrite, particularly in SW, where GG formed coordinated complexes with dissolved ions from both minerals. The results demonstrated that seawater exerts a baseline depressant effect on the pyrite surface, with the inclusion of guar gum providing an additional 21 percentage points of enhanced pyrite depression.
